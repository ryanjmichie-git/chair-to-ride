# Paratransit broker — dispatch policy

Dialysis riders travel on standing orders. Each treatment day the outbound leg is
booked to arrive before the chair start, and the return leg is anchored thirty
minutes after the scheduled session end.

Every trip gets a thirty-minute pickup window. The vehicle is on time if it arrives
anywhere inside that window; arriving outside it is a late trip and is recorded.

We may negotiate a requested time by up to sixty minutes either side. That is the
whole of our discretion. A trip that cannot be placed inside that band is not
scheduled; it is queued as a denial and the reason is logged for the record.

No vehicle picks anyone up before 05:30, when the fleet's shift starts.

A driver waits five minutes at the curb. After that the vehicle continues and the
rider is put back into the queue rather than left waiting for a vehicle that has gone.

Capacity is counted by mobility class at every stop, not per trip. A standard van
carries four ambulatory riders and one wheelchair. A lift van carries three
ambulatory riders and two wheelchairs. Neither carries a stretcher.

Stretcher trips are never auto-assigned. They go to a dispatcher, who books the
crew and the equipment by hand.

No rider spends more than sixty minutes on board, and no ride is longer than twice
the direct trip. If a shared route would break that, the pairing is dropped.

Nobody is stranded. Every return leg is either scheduled or sitting in the review
queue on will-call, and a will-call is a promise to come, not a cancellation.

Vehicles are on the road from 05:30 to 21:30. Outside those hours there is no service.
